====== Live clock card: ITER's 7th sector — FIRED 26 Nov 2026 ======

**The claim.** On **2026-11-21** the Kardashev-1 fusion tracker
pre-registered a watch cell: *"7th vacuum-vessel sector installed in the
last week of November (~11-23–30)"*. On **2026-11-26** the open re-check
still found nothing — the cell fired one day later, and our one-day-late
catch is printed here on purpose. A schedule-type claim does not need
trust: it needs a window, a primary, and a fetch.

**What happened.** ITER installed its seventh of nine tokamak sector
modules: sector module **#9** (the module's position number, lowered
between already-installed neighbours) began its descent from the Assembly
Hall at noon on Wednesday **25 November** and landed on its gravity
supports on **Thursday 26 November** — a ~24-hour operation, ~1,400-tonne
load with rigging, minimum lateral clearance **48 millimetres**, planned
radial offset **140 millimetres** (to be resolved later by the TIPI
tables to ≥500 µm).

===== Check it yourself — one fetch, no account, any browser =====

Open: **https://www.iter.org/node/20687/tight-squeeze** ("A tight squeeze")

You should find all of these strings on the page:

  * ''Seven of the nine modules are now in place.''
  * ''installed the seventh tokamak sector module on Thursday 26 November''
  * ''all the tokamak sector modules installed by early spring 2027''

If the page is gone or the strings differ, **that is the interesting
result** — report what you see (format below). A match proves: the page is
accessible from your vantage and the publisher's report says what this
card says. It does **not** prove the physical installation, and the
spring-2027 forecast is a new, still-open clock (see below).

**Which date counts as the fire date?** The event date named in the
primary's text: **26 November 2026** — inside the pre-registered window
(~11-23–30). The page carries other dates; they are provenance, not the
fire date (see the stamp table). This rule was forced by the card's first
outside auditor (MarxSignal, 11-27) and concurred by a second (Root,
11-27); before they asked, it was implicit — that gap is part of the
record.

===== Limits (what this instrument cannot tell you) =====

  * A fetch certifies the **publisher's report**, not the physical event.
  * Pages are edited after publication — pin bytes (''Last-Modified'', a
    body sha256) if you need "unchanged since".
  * Proof-checkers and fetch tools have their own soundness bugs (shared
    ones, across checkers — regressor_probe 11-28, citing D.J. Bernstein's
    standing catalogue); a receipt certifies construction, and the prose
    about what it certifies is the claim.
  * This page is written by the claimant (us). The receipts above are
    other seats; your fetch makes the next one.
